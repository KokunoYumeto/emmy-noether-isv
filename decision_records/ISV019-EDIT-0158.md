# ISV019-EDIT-0158 — integrated names, eponyms, and place names

Status at authoring: the source transaction has been applied once and independently replayed; metadata sealing and registry succession remain separate controlled steps.

## Decision

This edit adapts 66 morphologically integrated occurrences of twenty foreign-name, eponym, and place-name families to the project’s established Interslavic house spelling. It changes only `source_latin/base-papers1-43-isv.tex`. It does not change complete uninflected titles, bibliographic identities, quoted foreign text, mathematical payloads, comments, source locators, producer units, or `% Identity` receipts.

The governing distinction is grammatical integration. A name that takes Interslavic case, adjectival, eponymic, or place morphology is part of the readable Interslavic sentence and therefore receives a consistent Slavic-readable stem. A complete title, venue, citation, or uninflected identity remains foreign and is reserved for whole-island protection in the next tranche. This is a project-house editorial policy, not an official Interslavic proper-name standard.

## Authority and adverse evidence

The official orthography snapshot excludes `q`, `w`, and `x` from the standard Latin alphabet, excludes special borrowing letters such as German/French `ü`, recommends the most understandable spelling, and asks writers to remain consistent. The official dictionary snapshot is pinned only as a general lexical authority; no family here is represented as an official name headword.

Eight current exact Interslavic controls anchor the house system, including `Veber`, `Lurot`, `Enrikes`, `Ostrovski`, `Švarcovomu`, `Getingen`, `Klajn`, and `Štejnic`. The current cumulative Russian and Ukrainian editions are corroborating witnesses, not independent authority and not permission to copy East-Slavic spelling mechanically. They sometimes preserve original Latin citation spelling and sometimes differ in vowel adaptation.

The exhaustive post-EDIT0150 blocker-role manifest classified precisely these 66 occurrences as integrated names/eponyms needing a whole-family policy. Its separate class of complete foreign/title/venue/citation identities is not altered here.
