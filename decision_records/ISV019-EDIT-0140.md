## Decision

Correct exactly two signs in the assembled Paper 30 unit of the Latin base. In both places the sealed predecessor said that `n` was nonzero modulo `\mpideal`; German ED0015 and the primary printed article instead say that `n` is congruent to zero modulo `\mpideal`. The change removes only the two `\not` commands:

- base line 16909, relation column 36: `\mn\not\equiv0\pmod{\mpideal}` → `\mn\equiv0\pmod{\mpideal}`;
- base line 16912, relation column 15: `n\not\equiv0\pmod{\mpideal}` → `n\equiv0\pmod{\mpideal}`.

This is mathematical sign fidelity, not stylistic normalization. The proof needs the positive congruence: if `b` and `n` both belong to `\mpideal`, then `bn` belongs to `\mpideal^2`, contradicting `mc\not\equiv0\pmod{\mpideal^2}` because `bn=mc`. The predecessor's negative `n` congruence cannot supply that contradiction.
