## Decision

Correct exactly fifteen adjective/adverb uses whose inherited `javn-` wording says
“public/publicly” in the pinned official dictionary but whose German sentences mean
either “explicit” or “clear/evident.”  The repair is sentence-specific:

- seven explicit sites become the official `jednoznačn-` family;
- eight evident or inferential sites become official `jasno`;
- twelve unrelated `jav-` appear/manifest/publish verbs remain unchanged;
- the valid dictionary sense “public/publicly” remains available for future literal
  public senses.

This is a source-fidelity and false-friend repair supported by the active German
ED0015 and pinned official lexical/grammar evidence.  It is not native-speaker,
community, canonical, or critical-edition certification.

## Evidence and rejected alternatives

The fifteen active German controls are seven `explizit/expliziten`, seven
`offenbar`, and one `ersichtlich`.  The official snapshot distinguishes `javno`
(publicly) and `javny` (public) from `jednoznačno/jednoznačny`
(explicitly/unambiguously; explicit/unambiguous) and `jasno` (clearly/distinctly).
The official adjective grammar supplies regular adjective inflection and the
hard-stem adverb in `-o`.

Rejected: retaining `javn-` at these fifteen sites; mapping every site to `jasno`
and thereby losing explicitness; mapping every site to `jednoznačn-` and thereby
mislabeling eight inferences.  Held outside this edit: valid corpus `eksplicit-`
internationalisms and the separate `očivid-/očevid-` orthography/register family.
There are no semantic holds for the fifteen repaired sites.
